Harbor System is designed to detect and respond to raids automatically, but knowing how to act manually during an active incident lets you stop damage faster and make better decisions in the moment. This guide walks you through the incident response flow — from first detection to post-raid cleanup — so you can operate with confidence even under pressure.

## Signs of an Active Raid

Not all raids trigger Harbor's automated detection immediately. Watch for the following indicators:

- Harbor posts a raid detection alert in your log channel
- A sudden surge of new members joining within a short time window
- Spam appearing in multiple channels simultaneously
- Hateful, disruptive, or coordinated content being posted rapidly by new accounts
- Multiple accounts with similar usernames, profile pictures, or creation dates appearing in the member list

## Immediate Response

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the Log Channel">
    Navigate to your `#security-logs` channel immediately. Harbor posts a detection event with details including the accounts involved, join timestamps, and the action taken. Review this information before acting so you understand the scope of the raid.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify Lockdown is Active">
    Confirm that Harbor has engaged lockdown mode:

    ```
    /harbor anti-raid status
    ```

    The output will indicate whether a lockdown is currently active and which mode is in use.
  </Step>
  <Step title="If Not Auto-Locked, Lock Manually">
    If lockdown did not trigger automatically — for example, if the raid falls just below your threshold — restrict channel permissions manually in Discord while Harbor catches up. In Discord's channel settings, temporarily deny Send Messages for `@everyone` in your most active channels. Re-enable them once lockdown is confirmed active.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let Harbor Process Automated Actions">
    Harbor will kick or ban detected raiders based on your configured action setting. Do not manually act on the same accounts at the same time — conflicting actions can cause errors and make the audit log harder to review after the incident.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ban Remaining Raiders">
    For any raiders that slipped through Harbor's automated detection, issue manual bans:

    ```
    /harbor ban user:@Raider reason:"Raid participant"
    ```

    Using Harbor's ban command rather than Discord's native ban keeps the action logged within Harbor's audit system for easier post-incident review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Clear the Raid Flags">
    After the main wave is handled, check for any remaining flagged accounts that Harbor marked but did not automatically action:

    ```
    /harbor flags list
    ```

    Review each flagged account and take appropriate action — ban, kick, or dismiss the flag if it was a false positive.
  </Step>
  <Step title="End Lockdown">
    Once you are confident the raid is over and no further wave is coming, lift the lockdown and restore normal server operation:

    ```
    /harbor anti-raid unlock
    ```

    Verify that channels are accessible to legitimate members before announcing the all-clear to your community.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## After the Raid

Once the immediate threat is resolved, complete the following steps to restore normal operations and prevent recurrence:

- Review all log channel entries from the incident to build a complete picture of what happened
- Check if any legitimate members were caught in automated actions and reverse those decisions as needed:
  ```
  /harbor unban user:@Member
  ```
- Consider tightening your anti-raid thresholds if the raid was larger or faster than your configuration anticipated
- Enable member verification if it was not already active — it is the single most effective measure for stopping future raids at the door

<Note>
  Harbor logs all automated actions with reasons. If a legitimate member was accidentally actioned, their case will appear in the log with full context so you can reverse it.
</Note>

<Warning>
  Do not disable Harbor System during an active raid. Even if automated actions seem aggressive, disabling protection mid-raid will make the situation worse.
</Warning>
